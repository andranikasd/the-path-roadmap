    The loop, every phase

    1. Book the exam on day one, so the phase can't drag on.
    2. Watch and read one primary course or book, with a terminal open.
    3. Lab it in a throwaway VM, containerlab, kind or sandbox account.
    4. Build the project: small, complete and documented.
    5. Teach it in one post or talk. If you can't explain it, go back.
    6. Certify after two weeks of exam-style practice.

      What the review changed

      Exams spread out

      The first draft had an exam in each of nine straight months (M6–M14), on a 10–12 hour week. Core exams are now spaced so you only ever prepare for one, and later phases moved to fit.

      Optional exams made truly optional

      Pick at most two, and only for a clear reason. The C and Python certificates carry little weight at this level, and projects prove more.

      Two topics added

      Algorithms, which senior interview loops still test, and AI infrastructure, which platform teams are increasingly asked to run.

      Paid courses have free alternatives

      Every paid course (Cantrill, KodeKloud) now has a free video option. Paid practice exams are still worth it close to exam day.

      Factual fixes

      TLPI predates namespaces and cgroups, so those now point to the man pages and LWN. go-fuzz is replaced by native Go fuzzing. System design proof now points at the design docs project.

      Thin coverage is labelled

      A few topics have no strong free course. They're marked "Thin coverage" rather than padded out, so lean on the book or docs for those.
